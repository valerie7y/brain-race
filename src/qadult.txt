puzzle|How many times can you subtract 5 from 25?|It's a word trick.|After the first time, it's not 25.|Once (after that you're subtracting from 20)|Riddles like this exploit how quickly we jump to arithmetic.
puzzle|"Brothers and sisters I have none, but that man's father is my father's son." Who is "that man"?|"My father's son" is the speaker.|So that man's father is the speaker.|The speaker's son|A classic logic riddle that's appeared in puzzle books since the 1800s.
puzzle|What comes next: 1, 11, 21, 1211, 111221, ...?|Read each term out loud.|"One 1," "two 1s," ...|312211|John Conway proved that the length of this "look-and-say" sequence grows by about 30% each step.
puzzle|What is the sum of the integers from 1 to 100?|Pair 1 with 100, 2 with 99...|50 pairs of 101.|5,050|Legend says young Carl Friedrich Gauss found this shortcut as a schoolboy.
puzzle|In the Monty Hall problem, should you switch doors, and what is your chance of winning if you do?|Monty knows where the car is.|Switching wins whenever you first picked a goat.|Yes, switch: 2/3 chance|Even the mathematician Paul Erdős doubted the answer until he saw a computer simulation.
